Hidden Uncertainty in ACS Median Home Value Data

A study illustrating the exact magnitude of error in American Community Survey Median Home Value estimates through manually calculated county, census tract, and block group level data.

Overview

What were we trying to find out?

We wanted to determine how well ACS 5-year Median Home Value (MHV) estimates represent actual housing values. Specifically, we asked whether ACS estimates accurately reflect the residential landscape and how closely they compare with authoritative property-assessment data.

Our Approach and Methods

We collected property-assessment data from Richmond City, Henrico County, and Powhatan County, representing urban, suburban, and rural areas. We cleaned and inflation-adjusted 2018–2024 assessment data, removed non-residential and incomplete records, and calculated median home values by Census tract and block group.

In Richmond City, we also identified owner-occupied parcels to create a closer comparison with the ACS. We compared our parcel-based estimates against ACS 1-year and 5-year MHV estimates, examining percentage error, ACS margins of error, and correlations between the two datasets.

Our Results

ACS estimates showed strong correlations with parcel-based values, generally capturing the spatial pattern of housing values. However, the estimates often differed substantially in magnitude. Only 17.4% of tract comparisons and 29.2% of block-group comparisons fell within the ACS margin of error, while parcel-based estimates were generally lower than ACS estimates.

What I Learned

The key takeaway was that strong correlation does not necessarily mean accurate agreement. ACS MHV can be useful for identifying broad spatial patterns, but researchers should be cautious about treating it as a precise measure of neighborhood housing values.

The project also showed the value—and limitations—of using administrative data to evaluate survey-based estimates. Data availability and locality-specific record structures strongly influence what questions can actually be answered.
